# Backlog: the Intel 8080 and 8085

Status: **CPUs, instruction set, native assembly, disassembly and hover help done (2026-11-13); no machine
yet**. Intel's 8-bit CPUs after the 8008, the line the Z80 extends.

## What is built

[`Retrocore.Cpu.I8080`](../../src/Retrocore.Cpu.I8080) holds both CPUs, as `Retrocore.Cpu.M6502` holds the 6502
family:

1. **The instruction set as data** ([`I8080Isa.txt`](../../src/Retrocore.Cpu.I8080/I8080Isa.txt)): `[I8080]` and
   `[I8085]`, each listing every opcode with its states, since almost every timing differs between the two. It
   drives the assembler, disassembler, flow analysis, hover help and the VS Code grammar.
2. **The native syntax is Intel's** (`MOV A,M`, `LXI H,Table`, `PUSH PSW`, `RST 7`), with the expression syntax
   every native source shares. `RST` takes the restart number, a new `rstn` operand field held in the opcode.
3. **A state-exact core**, `I8080Processor<TBus>`, one class for both chips, chosen by `I8080Variant`. Each
   machine cycle drives its address on T1, samples READY on T2 and moves its byte on T3; M1 decodes on T4,
   and the 8080's 5-state and the 8085's 6-state M1s add idle states. The 8085 skips the high address byte of a
   jump or call not taken (7 and 9 states). Snapshots restore mid-instruction.
4. **Interrupts.** INT/INTR with the device's opcode from `InterruptData`, `EI`'s one-instruction delay, and on
   the 8085 TRAP, RST 7.5 (edge latch), 6.5 and 5.5 with their priority and `SIM` masks, and `RIM` reading IE as
   TRAP found it.
5. **Analysis** follows the restarts on both CPUs and, on the 8085 alone, the TRAP and RST 5.5, 6.5 and 7.5
   entries (`entry-points in I8085`); `RSTV` records a conditional call to `$40` (`target RSTV 0040`).

## How it is verified

- The four classic CP/M programs pass on the 8080: TST8080, 8080PRE, SuperSoft's CPUTEST and every one of
  8080EXM's 25 CRC tests (every flag bit). TST8080 and 8080PRE also pass on the 8085.
  [`Fetch-I8080Tests.ps1`](../../tools/Fetch-I8080Tests.ps1) pins them, from superzazu/8080, outside the
  workspace.
- Every opcode of both CPUs takes the states the description lists, taken and not taken, and round-trips through
  the disassembler and assembler.
- Focused tests for bus timing, READY, ports, interrupts, `RIM`/`SIM`, and the 8085's undocumented instructions.
- The 8085's undocumented flags were compared (2026-11-13) with two independent emulators, run side by side on
  2.7 million cases: [V85](https://github.com/EtchedPixels/V85) at `01db39d`, executed, and MAME's `i8085` at
  `98ff9fd`, read. Neither is copied into the repository.

  | Instructions | This core | V85 | MAME |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | 8-bit add, subtract, compare, `INR`, `DCR`: V and K | V from the carries into and out of bit 7, K = V xor S | Agrees on all 2.1 million cases once a sign-extension bug in its `calc_Vadd`/`calc_Vsub` (`int8_t` operands) is fixed | Does not model them: sets bit 1 after subtracts, clears K |
  | Logical operations | V clear, K = S | Agrees | Clears both |
  | `ANA` AC | Set, as Intel's 8085 manual says | 8080 rule | Set |
  | `INX`, `DCX` | K on the wrap, V unchanged | Also sets V on `$7FFF`/`$8000` | K on the wrap, V unchanged |
  | `DAD`, rotates | CY only | Also V and K | CY only |
  | `DSUB` | All flags from the high byte but Z, which covers the word | Agrees but Z, from the high byte | Z over the word; clears P and K |
  | `RDEL` | CY, and V when bit 15 changes | Differs in 24 cases (its own "FIXME") | Agrees |
  | `ARHL`, `LDHI`, `LDSI` | CY only, no flags | Agrees | Agrees |
  | `DAA` V and K | From the internal add of the correction | Its README calls them wrong | Leaves them unchanged |

  `EightBitArithmeticSetsVAndKBySignedOverflow` checks the first row over every input against the signed range,
  and `UndocumentedFlagsWhereReferencesDiffer` pins the rows where the references part.

## Decisions (2026-11-13)

- **8080 and 8085 now; the 8008 later.** The 8008 is a different design (14-bit addresses, an on-chip stack, its
  own opcodes) and would be a description and core of its own.
- **The 8085's V and K flags** follow Ken Shirriff's
  [silicon analysis](https://www.righto.com/2013/07/reverse-engineering-flag-circuits-in.html): V is the
  exclusive-or of the carries into and out of bit 7, K is V exclusive-or S, and `INX` and `DCX` set K alone, on a
  wrap from `$FFFF` to 0 or 0 to `$FFFF`. Logical operations clear V.
- **The 8085's AC** after `ANA` is always set; on the 8080 it is bit 3 of the operands ORed. AC after a subtract
  is the carry out of bit 3 of the complement addition on both, as the exercisers confirm for the 8080.

## Open

- **Still unsettled on the 8085**, where the references disagree and no hardware trace exists: V and K after
  `DAA`, `DAD` and the rotates; `DSUB`'s Z; and the states of TRAP and RST 5.5-7.5, modelled as 6 idle states
  before the two pushes (12 in all, as V85 counts them; MAME counts 11). A logic-analyser trace of a real 8085
  would settle them.
- **Interrupt acknowledge** takes a single opcode; an 8259-style three-byte `CALL` would read its operands from
  memory. HOLD is not modelled, and RESET acts on its rising edge, as the Z80's does.
- **TRAP is taken on its rising edge alone.** The 8085 also needs TRAP still high when it samples it, so a pulse
  that ends before the instruction does is taken here but not on the chip.
- **Coverage gaps.** CPUTEST has not been run on the 8085, and neither CPU has a benchmark in
  [`Retrocore.Perf`](../../tests/Retrocore.Perf) like the Z80's and the 6809's.
- **Intel-dialect source** (`EQU`, `DB`, `1234H`) is a [dialect](Dialects.md#8080-and-8085-dialects) to add.
- **The 8085 has no machine yet**; the 8080 debugs in [the Altair 8800](Altair8800.md).
